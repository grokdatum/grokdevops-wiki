## Objective
Add a readiness probe to prevent traffic from hitting pods too early

## Hint 1
🔍 Observation Hint:

Check the pods:
`kubectl get pods -n k8squest -l app=slow-app`

Do you notice the READY column? Some pods might show "0/1" even though STATUS is "Running".

Check the service endpoints:
`kubectl get endpoints slow-app-service -n k8squest`

Are unready pods being added to the service? This would send traffic to pods that aren't ready yet!

Try describing a pod that's not ready:
`kubectl describe pod <pod-name> -n k8squest`

## Hint 2
🧭 Direction Hint:

The problem: Pods are Running, but they take 20 seconds to actually be ready to serve traffic (simulated with sleep 20 in postStart hook).

Without a readiness probe, Kubernetes assumes "Running = Ready" and immediately adds the pod to the Service endpoints.

This means users hit pods that are still starting up → 502 Bad Gateway errors!

Check the deployment:
`kubectl get deployment slow-startup-app -n k8squest -o yaml | grep -A 10 readinessProbe`

You'll likely see nothing (or just livenessProbe). That's the problem!

## Hint 3
💡 Near-Solution Hint:

You need to add a readiness probe that waits for the app to actually be ready.

Steps:
1. Edit the deployment:
   `kubectl edit deployment slow-startup-app -n k8squest`

2. Find the `livenessProbe` section, and add a `readinessProbe` right below it:

```yaml
        readinessProbe:
          httpGet:
            path: /
            port: 80
          initialDelaySeconds: 22  # Wait for the 20s sleep + 2s buffer
          periodSeconds: 5
          successThreshold: 1
```

3. Save and exit. Pods will be recreated.

4. Watch the pods become ready:
   `kubectl get pods -n k8squest -l app=slow-app -w`

Now pods won't receive traffic until they're actually ready!

## Objective
Fix either the Service selector or Pod labels so traffic can reach the pod

## Hint 1
🔍 Observation Hint:

First, check if the pod is running: `kubectl get pod backend-app -n k8squest`

Then check the service: `kubectl get service backend-service -n k8squest`

Finally, check if the service found any pods: `kubectl get endpoints backend-service -n k8squest`

Do you see any endpoints (IP addresses)? If not, the service isn't connected to the pod.

## Hint 2
🧭 Direction Hint:

The service finds pods using **label selectors**. The service's selector must match the pod's labels.

Check the service selector:
`kubectl get service backend-service -n k8squest -o yaml | grep -A 3 selector:`

Check the pod labels:
`kubectl get pod backend-app -n k8squest --show-labels`

Do the labels match? Find the mismatch!

## Hint 3
💡 Near-Solution Hint:

The service selector has `app: frontend` but the pod has label `app: backend`. They don't match!

Steps to fix:
1. Delete the resources: `kubectl delete -f broken.yaml`
2. Edit broken.yaml and change the service selector from `app: frontend` to `app: backend`
3. Apply: `kubectl apply -f broken.yaml`
4. Verify endpoints exist: `kubectl get endpoints backend-service -n k8squest`

You should see an IP address listed as an endpoint!

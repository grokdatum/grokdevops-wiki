## Objective
Adjust the resource requests so the pod can be scheduled

## Hint 1
🔍 Observation Hint:

Check the pod status: `kubectl get pod hungry-app -n k8squest`

You'll see it's stuck in "Pending" state. Run `kubectl describe pod hungry-app -n k8squest` and look at the Events section.

What message do you see about scheduling?

## Hint 2
🧭 Direction Hint:

The Events show something like "0/1 nodes are available: 1 Insufficient cpu" or "Insufficient memory".

This means the pod is requesting more resources than any node in your cluster can provide.

Check what resources the pod is requesting:
`kubectl get pod hungry-app -n k8squest -o yaml | grep -A 4 resources:`

Compare this to available node capacity:
`kubectl describe nodes | grep -A 5 Allocatable`

## Hint 3
💡 Near-Solution Hint:

The pod is requesting 999 CPUs and 999Gi of memory—way more than any node has!

Steps to fix:
1. Delete the pod: `kubectl delete pod hungry-app -n k8squest`
2. Edit broken.yaml and change the resource requests to reasonable values:
   - memory: "64Mi" (instead of 999Gi)
   - cpu: "100m" (instead of 999)
3. Apply: `kubectl apply -f broken.yaml`
4. Verify: `kubectl get pod hungry-app -n k8squest`

The pod should now be scheduled and running!
